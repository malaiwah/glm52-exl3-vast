# GG v20-r28 / GLM-5.2 3.42-bpw qualification

AIBeast qualified the r28 turnkey appliance on 2026-08-06 using four RTX PRO
6000 Blackwell GPUs at a 280 W/card limit, driver 595.71.05, CUDA 13.2, and
deterministic physical ordering `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=2,1,0,3`.

The checkpoint was mounted read-only from NFS. All 83 SHA256-addressed LFS
objects matched the pinned Hugging Face revision. Runtime logs selected

at layers 4–77, and selected the r28 lossless TP4/DCP4 policy.

## Why the KV pool is pinned

Natural GMU-0.95 profiling exposed 524,800 logical tokens and left about
2.3 GiB/GPU immediately after startup. It was not workload-safe: the first
128K prefill requested a 48 MiB mixed-Trellis temporary while only 32.81 MiB
was free and the engine OOMed. Pinning 2,032 blocks returned eighteen blocks
of execution headroom and exposed exactly 520,192 logical tokens. The same
128K request then passed at 2,214.6 tok/s.

PP and C1 are below the original aspirational 2,400/100 tok/s targets. The
owner accepted this trade after the correctness, context, stability, and
quality gates passed. These throughput targets remain optimization goals, not
release blockers for this higher-fidelity checkpoint.

### Draft-policy A/B

A matched greedy-MTP3 arm produced PP 2,394 / 2,153 / 2,189 tok/s and
aggregate decode 76.7 / 125.5 / 153.5 tok/s at C1/C4/C8. Its mean acceptance
length was 2.54 / 1.99 / 1.89. Greedy therefore helped solo decode but lost
15.9% at C4 and 11.5% at C8 versus probabilistic drafting. The production
profile pins `probabilistic`, which is the better agent/concurrency trade.

MTP5 was rejected before an expensive full reload. With eight sequences it
requires a 48-query capture/trellis window instead of 32; the stock shape
correctly fails validation until `VLLM_EXL3_TRELLIS_MAX_M`, the maximum graph
size, and every capture size are raised to 48. That larger arena consumes the
execution margin recovered by the 2,032-block pin, so it cannot honestly be
combined with the workload-proven 520,192-token envelope on this host. MTP3
remains the context-safe selection.

## Correctness and maximum context

All API gates passed: ordinary and thinking chat, reasoning visibility,
streaming usage, preserved-thinking multi-turn, structured JSON with and
without thinking, tool calls and tool-result round trip, tokenization, and the
`GLM-5.2` plus `local-primary` aliases. This checkpoint is text-only.

The salted needle matrix recovered every fact at five depths (1%, 25%, 50%,
75%, 99%) for each prompt size: 8K, 32K, 96K, 128K, 162K, 256K, 384K,
507,904, and 516,096 tokens. The final prompt plus a 4,096-token generation
reserve proves the full 520,192-token envelope: 45/45 retrievals, zero OOMs,
zero preemptions, and no degeneration. This directly refutes a 162K hard
corruption ceiling for this lossless profile.

## KLD and the KV tradeoff

Each KLD arm used the same 2,048-token BF16-logit reference (SHA256
`87f992a689c054a0548a4b3863da6c809f9239beacd5786d0401e45904fec063`)
and all 2,047 teacher-forced positions:

| serving form | mean KLD | offline model VRAM/GPU |
|---|---:|---:|
| pre-window r26 3.36-bpw K6 + dynamic NVFP4 repeat | 0.082507 | — |
| native weights + dynamic NVFP4/FP8-RoPE KV | 0.082039 | 83.73 GiB |
| online K6 + dynamic NVFP4/FP8-RoPE KV | 0.089888 | 79.13 GiB |
| online K6 + FP8/BF16-RoPE KV | 0.077949 | 79.13 GiB |

Online K6 saves 4.60 GiB/GPU in the isolated MTP0/eager load shape at a
measured +0.007849 absolute KLD cost under dynamic NVFP4. Holding K6 fixed,
FP8 KV improves KLD by 0.011939, but its 656-byte record reduces practical
context to about 295K versus 520K with the 368-byte dynamic-NVFP4 record.
Dynamic NVFP4 remains production because usable context is the hard Hermes
Agents requirement. The reference bundle has one window, so a zero standard
deviation would be structural (`n=1`), not a model-variance claim.

The requested “current baseline” repeat used 3.36 bpw because that—not the
older 3.25-bpw profile—was the stack actually serving immediately before this
maintenance window. Its 0.082507 result is retained above rather than silently
substituting a stale model label.

## Startup and cache behavior

A cold weight/K6 pass took 3,815 seconds and mixed-Trellis hydration added
about 25 minutes. A warm restart reused every deterministic K6 artifact and
completed model loading in 699.7 seconds. A separate KLD launch accidentally
mounted an empty cache root and immediately began re-encoding; it was stopped.
Mounting the serving cache produced cache hits even when KV changed from NVFP4
to FP8, confirming that KV format is not part of the weight-overlay identity.
The local runner gives this profile a 90-minute health-start grace; other
profiles retain 45 minutes.

The final production promotion started at 21:12:04 UTC and became healthy on
port 8000 at 21:27:36: 15 minutes 31 seconds. NFS shard loading took 417
seconds on this restart; every K6 and AOT artifact was a cache hit. It exposed
both `GLM-5.2` and `local-primary`, completed a visible-content chat smoke,
reported zero restarts, and left 2,593 MiB physically free per GPU at idle.
External agent traffic returned successfully during the initial soak. LMCache
then reported a 256-chunk NVMe-to-DRAM prefetch hit at 10.29 GB/s, 1.14 GB in
L1, and 99.49 GB used in its bounded 512 GiB L2 tier.

A final production C1 smoke processed an uncached 65,536-token prompt at
2,332.6 tok/s with zero error, OOM, preemption, or restart. During that prefill
all four cards sustained their 280 W cap and retained 505 MiB physically free.
Physical GPU 2 remained the coldest card at 43 °C versus 45/48/52 °C, so the
deterministic `2,1,0,3` order correctly keeps rank 0 on the coldest slot.

During the subsequent agent-traffic soak, ten-second log windows observed up
to 2,515 tok/s prompt processing and about 110 tok/s generation with one or two
requests active; MAL commonly reached 3.1--3.4. These are traffic-polluted
operational peaks, not substitutes for the matched benchmark table, but they
show that the selected profile can cross the original 2,400 PP / 100 TG goals
on real work. No error, OOM, preemption, or restart appeared during the soak.
